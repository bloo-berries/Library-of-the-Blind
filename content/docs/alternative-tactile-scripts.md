Some blind users prefer or require scripts that differ from traditional Braille. These are legitimate entries for an inclusive library.

## Pre-Braille Embossed Letter Systems (Late 18th-19th Century)

Historical tactile reading systems that predate or competed with Braille, using raised letter forms.

- **Hauy's Embossed Roman Letters** (France, 1784) - First systematic tactile reading system
- **Snider's System** (USA, 1834) - First embossed book in the United States
- **Boston Line Type** (USA, 1835) - Standard at Perkins School for the Blind
- **Philadelphia Line Type** (USA, mid-19th century) - Used at Pennsylvania Institution
- **Gall's Triangular Alphabet** (Scotland, 1826) - First embossed books in English
- **Alston Type** (UK, 1830s) - Printed whole Bible in 1840
- **Klein's Stachel-Schrift** (Austria, 1807) - Pin Script using raised Roman letters

## Dot-Based Alternative Systems

- **Night Writing (Sonography)** (France, 1815-1820s) - Charles Barbier's 12-dot system, precursor to Braille
- **Standard Braille** (France, 1824-present) - Louis Braille's 6-dot cell system, international standard
- **Raphigraphy / Decapoint** (France, 1839) - First digital font, readable by both blind and sighted
- **Ballu's Decapoint** (Spain, 19th century) - 10-dot writing system

## Modern Alternative Scripts

- **Moon Type** (UK, 1845-present) - Simplified raised-outline characters, easier for those with reduced sensitivity
- **New York Point** (USA, 1868-1916) - Variable-width cells, now obsolete
- **ELIA Frames** (USA, 2000s-present) - Geometric shapes for late-blind adults
- **Fakoo** (2006-2008) - 3x3 dot grid readable by both blind and sighted
- **Quadoo** (2008) - Square-based alternative to Moon alphabet
- **Siekoo** (2012) - For seven-segment electronic displays

## Finger-Spelling and Manual Alphabets

- **Lorm Alphabet** (Austria, 1881-present) - Touch-based system using palm locations
- **Malossi Alphabet** (Italy, 20th century) - Tapping/pinching finger joints
- **Spartan Alphabet / Block Alphabet** - Capital letters drawn on palm
- **Tadoma** (USA, early 20th century) - Tactile lip-reading with hand on speaker's face
- **Polish Tactile Alphabet** (Poland, 1980s-present) - Signs, lines, circles on hand
- **Braille Signing / Palm Braille** - Six spots on palm represent Braille cell

## DeafBlind Communication Systems

- **Pro-Tactile (PT)** - Tactile sign language using pressure and movement
- **Haptic Communication** (Scandinavia) - Systematic touch signals on back/arm
- **Social Haptic Communication** (Finland) - 139+ standardized signals
- **TaSSeLS** - Tactile Signing for Sensory Learners
- **Visual Frame Sign Language** - Signing within defined visual frame
- **Canaan Barrie On-Body Signing** (Scotland) - For children with multiple disabilities

## Tactile Sign Language Adaptations

- **Tactile ASL** (USA)
- **Tactile BSL** (UK)
- **Tactile Israeli Sign Language** (Israel)
- **Tactile JSL** (Japan)
- And many more regional adaptations

## Ancient and Historical Systems

- **Didymus the Blind's Carved Letters** (Egypt, 4th century CE) - Earliest documented tactile reading
- **Quipu / Khipu** (Andes, 2600 BCE-present) - Knot-based recording system
- **Japanese Anma Tradition** (1600s-present) - Tactile massage practice
- **Father Lana's Code** (Italy, 1617) - 3x3 dot matrix, proto-Braille

## Notes

- These systems serve different needs and communities
- Some are historical but important for understanding accessibility evolution
- Pro-Tactile is particularly important for the DeafBlind community
- Moon Type remains in use, especially for those who lose sight later in life
